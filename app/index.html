P25 Phase 1 conventional simplex. One C4FM channel, half duplex. Listen in this browser, or send the 1011 Hz test tone. These fields stay on this computer.

The HackRF transmits only while push-to-talk is held, and it stops at 30 seconds. The RF amplifier stays off. Use a frequency you are allowed to transmit on. Keep the radios close together, or put a dummy load on the HackRF.

Listen plays clear voice in this browser. Click it again to stop. Push to talk stops listening and sends the test tone. Release it and listening returns. Stop goes idle and frees the HackRF.

Help

Channel

This panel is one conventional Phase 1 voice channel. The emission is C4FM, 12.5 kHz, FDMA. Phase 2 TDMA, trunking, scanning, and encryption keys are outside this panel. The call is a group call. The voice frames are clear IMBE, algorithm ID 80 hex, key ID 0. A radio with encryption off can decode them with its own IMBE decoder.

Listen

Listen opens the HackRF at 2 million samples per second and runs the CQPSK demodulator this build uses for Phase 1 C4FM. The vocoder turns clear IMBE into 8000 Hz audio, and this tab plays it. Leave the tab open.

The local oscillator is 50 kHz above the frequency you typed, so the tuner’s DC spike stays off the carrier. The waterfall is 2 MHz wide and centered on your frequency. The white line is the channel. The red line is the local oscillator. If the thin voice trace sits above the white line, raise PPM. If it sits below, lower PPM. The red line itself is the 50 kHz offset, and PPM will not move it onto the white line.

Start listen gain at 16. Raise it when the waterfall stays dark and no voice decodes. Lower it when the display turns into a bright band. The status line shows the NAC and talkgroup taken from decoded voice frames. A simplex channel sends no trunking control messages, so a control-channel timeout in the log is normal.

Transmit

Push to talk sends the standard 1011 Hz Phase 1 test tone. That tone is an IMBE codeword, repeated until you release the button. Every frame carries the NAC, talkgroup, and source ID typed here. The microphone is unused. The HackRF is half duplex, so listening stops before the tone goes out, and release returns to listen.

GNU Radio takes a few seconds to start. Keep holding after the status says transmitting. The tone then runs until you release, or until the 30 second limit. During transmit the local oscillator is on the channel, so the DC spike is on the carrier. The waterfall pauses because the receiver is stopped.

Transmit IF gain is an amplifier step, from 0 to 40 dB in steps of 8. The RF amplifier, about 11 dB, stays off. HackRF has no calibrated dBm control. With the RF amplifier off, 0 and 16 are below 0 dBm (1 milliwatt). A setting near 40 can land around 0 dBm, and the real level still changes with frequency. The board’s maximum from 10 MHz to 2170 MHz is about 5 to 15 dBm, which takes the RF amplifier on and IF gain at 47. This panel never does that. On the bench, use 0 or 16, with the radios close or a dummy load fitted.

The other radio lights and stays quiet

  • Match the frequency. A receive light can come from carrier energy before the voice frames decode.
  • Match the NAC. $F7E on that radio accepts any NAC. Typing F7E here transmits F7E, and a radio set to 293 will refuse it. Type F7E here when you want this receiver to accept every NAC.
  • Leave encryption off on that radio. These frames are clear, algorithm 80 hex.
  • Selective unmute also requires this talkgroup. Normal unmute follows the NAC and ignores the talkgroup.
  • The source ID is the sending unit. A group call plays when the NAC matches, even when this ID is different from the receiving radio.
  • If the light is on and the speaker is quiet at transmit IF gain 0, try 32 with the radios close together.
